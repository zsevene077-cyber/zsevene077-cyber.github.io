VLA evaluation: making experiments comparable

I contribute to embodied-model evaluation through concurrency tuning, inference micro-batching, and result auditing, examining throughput, resource use, and evaluation completeness together.

LIBERO · RoboTwin2 · GPU · Python

The question

How can evaluation finish sooner while keeping its experimental conditions and results auditable?

My contribution

  • Run and monitor evaluations in simulation environments including LIBERO and RoboTwin2, following model services, workers, and result artifacts.
  • Compare evaluator concurrency and inference micro-batching, recording elapsed time, memory, utilization, and failures rather than relying only on service readiness.
  • Audit episode identity and coverage, detect duplicates and omissions, and preserve failures and recovery records.

Conditions and scope

A July 2026 comparison of DM0 on LIBERO Spatial: 10 tasks × 50 episodes, seed 7, with one RTX 4090D for inference. The configuration changes from 2 evaluator workers without batching to 10 workers with batch 4 and a 20 ms wait. The 55.8- and 32.0-minute values use the wall-time measure in that experiment record.

How to interpret the result

This is a single-seed performance result for a specific model, environment, and configuration. It is not a model success-rate improvement or a universal speedup. Higher concurrency also requires a memory trade-off.

What I learned

I increasingly treat completion as a set of checks: the run has terminated, artifacts are saved, coverage is complete, and errors are accounted for. Performance comes after those checks.
